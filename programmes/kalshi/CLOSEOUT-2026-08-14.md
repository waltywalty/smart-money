## 1. Retention — what is now known, and what is still assumed

**Known.** Kalshi splits its data into a **live** set and a **historical** set at a boundary that
can be queried: `GET /historical/cutoff` returned `market_settled_ts: 2026-06-14T00:00:00Z`.
Markets settling before it are served from a parallel endpoint family — `/historical/markets`,
`/historical/markets/{ticker}/candlesticks`, `/historical/trades`, `/historical/fills`,
`/historical/orders`, `/historical/positions`. This was verified against events that return zero
rows from `/markets`: `/historical/markets?event_ticker=HIGHNY-21AUG06` returns a market from
**2021** with its settlement result, and `/historical/markets/KXHIGHNY-26JUN03-T83/candlesticks`
returns candles where the live path 404s. Kalshi documents it directly. Separately, the **live**
set's floor is real and time-based: by `close_time` it sat at 2026-06-08 on 2026-08-14, identical
across sixteen series carrying between 25 and 6,348 events, and one day later than the day before.
Uniformity across three orders of magnitude of event volume excludes count-based expiry. KXRAIN's
apparent anomaly is series age — 24 events in total, cursor exhausted, earliest `KXRAIN-26AUG01`.

**Assumed.** That the live floor keeps advancing a day per day — two timepoints give a direction,
not a law, and the ten-day census exists to convert that into a measurement. That `/historical`
has no depth limit of its own; 2021 is the oldest thing tested, not a demonstrated floor. That
unauthenticated access reaches as far as authenticated, which is untested because this project
holds no Kalshi credentials. And that `/historical/cutoff`'s `market_settled_ts` tracks the
observed `close_time` floor rather than merely being consistent with it once — today they read
2026-06-14 and 2026-06-08, which is compatible since markets settle after they close, but that is
an inference from a single pairing.

## 5. What could not be completed, and why

**T2's row-level data file.** There is no file channel between the Kernel VM, which can reach
Kalshi, and the Cowork sandbox, which holds the repo. Kalshi is 403 from Cowork; GitHub's API is
reachable from Kernel but the stored PAT returns 401. Moving 13,832 rows through the conversation
was attempted — encoded to 87 KB — and abandoned when hand re-emitting base64 corrupted on the
first chunk. Counts, the second-endpoint cross-check and per-series concentration were computed
and are recorded. **A fresh GitHub PAT with `contents:write` would let the Kernel VM commit
directly and retire the entire patch-and-apply cycle.** It is the single highest-value unblock.

**H64's depth check.** The pre-registration required depth beside every price. Candlesticks carry
volume, not resting size, so *"what fraction of qualifying rungs had size at the ask"* is
unanswered. `archive.pmxt.dev` holds the book and would answer it. Until it runs, **every H64
figure describes a quote, not a fill.**

**T4b's conclusion.** By design — nine more censuses to go.

## 6. H64, since it finished after the packet

**COULD NOT ESTABLISH.** +0.5135¢ per event, 95% bootstrap [−1.4469, +2.1816], n=245 events / 254
rungs. The 150-event bar was **cleared**, so this is a genuine null rather than an underpowered
one. Leave-one-series-out crosses zero (−0.2227 to +1.0963; one series carrying 20.1% of rungs
flips the sign) and the pre-committed split-half does not replicate.

Two things it does establish. **H55's structural finding is resolved** — 6.22% of usable markets
sat in the 93–98¢ band at T−10m against 0.07% at T−24h, about 89× better populated, so the
`revive_if` pointed at the right place and there was nothing there. And **the motivating premise is
defeated by rounding**: all 254 rungs were charged exactly 1 cent against a theoretical 0.2917¢, a
ratio of 3.43×, because Kalshi rounds fees up to whole cents on the order total. The ceiling binds
hardest exactly where the formula is smallest. **H55's `revive_if` is discharged.**
