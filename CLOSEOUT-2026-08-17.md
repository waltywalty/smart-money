## 6. The two paragraphs that decide packet 4

### What A1 established about the fee ceiling

The ceiling is **per fill and to a centicent**, not per order and not to a whole cent. The
whole-cent behaviour this project has quoted for weeks is real but comes from somewhere else — a
balance-precision rounding fee of **$0.01 for a non-direct member**, partly rebated by a per-order
accumulator. All three of Kalshi's documented worked examples reproduce exactly under that model.
The practical consequence is that **fragmentation is free and size is not**: 100 contracts at
p=0.97 cost 0.2100¢ each whether filled in 1, 5 or 20 pieces, while a single contract at the same
price costs 1.0000¢ — **4.76×**, rising to **14.29×** at p=0.99. What A1 could **not** establish is
the base rate: Kalshi's own example gives \$0.0085 for one contract at \$0.055 where
`0.07·p·(1−p)` gives \$0.003638, so `rate` remains a visible parameter in every calculation. And
C1 found the sting in the tail: **not one registry entry states the order size its cost model
assumed**, because every model in this project is the size-free continuous function. The registry
has been pricing at a fee only reachable at size — the opposite of the error the packet expected —
and the hurdle every kill calibrates against inherits that (**P11**).

### What A2 established about historical reach

Historical depth is **not a constraint**. `/historical/markets` reaches each series' launch —
2021-07-26 for KXFED — and the cursor exhausts every time, so the limit is data rather than
pagination or the API; candlesticks reach exactly as deep, 18 of 18 tickers back to 2021, with
identical counts on both paths where they overlap. B1 turned that into an asset:
**7,269,014 settled markets across 6,703 series**, row count equal to unique-ticker count exactly,
verified against individual re-fetches on 9,814 markets with zero mismatches. What A2 also
established is that **the two paths are not redundant and neither is complete**: live `/markets`
has a `close_time` floor sliding one day per day, `/historical` stops at the cutoff, and the union
is the universe. Today they overlap by six days; that band is the only place a cross-check is
possible, and if the floor keeps advancing while the cutoff stays frozen they cross on about
**2026-08-20** and a reachability hole opens (**P9**). Two traps sit inside the good news:
`open_interest_fp` reads `0.00` on the historical path for every market tested, and `ticker=` is
silently ignored as a filter on both paths — the second was caught only because an impossible
control key was run in the same pass, after it had already produced six confident, identical,
entirely fabricated close times.
